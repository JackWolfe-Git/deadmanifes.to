The deadmanifesto

Philosophy of the Deadman

DEADMANIFESTO

I don't want to watch the AI era happen. I want to participate in it.

1. The barrier fell

AI is already capable of an absurd amount. We're still in the very early stages of the era, but the barrier between having an idea and attempting it has dropped dramatically for an individual. You can have an idea in the morning and potentially a working version by the evening. That changes the practical scale of what one person can realistically try.

2. What “build” means to me

To build is to take control of a system and actualise an idea. Generating is an automatic process where the person does not intentionally understand or control the underlying process.

For me, building is a mindset shift towards controlled applications of AI-assisted coding and logic. AI can save time on research and manual effort, but the human remains in control of the workflow, decisions and intended outcome.

3. AI-assisted vs AI-generated

AI-assisted: specific designs, methods or problems are given to the system, which helps with decision-making and carries out actions, while I establish the goals and direct the workflow.

AI-generated: the model directly synthesises the finished result from a prompt, particularly in generated video, audio or imagery, without me determining the underlying construction or logic.

Non-AI work: no-one in the process used AI at any point.

4. Building proves the point

Two projects crystallise this for me: the operating-system configuration I'd been thinking about for ages, and this website built from scratch. The tools didn't make them possible because AI did all the work; they made them possible because I could finally attempt things I'd wanted to do but couldn't on my own.

5. Augmentation, not substitution

I dislike using generated image, video and audio as a replacement for human creative work. I don't want a synthetic representation of a real person presented as that person — that's substitution. I'm open to generated media being part of a controlled, human-led process, which is augmentation. The distinction is augmentation vs substitution.

6. Responsibility, attribution, ethical use

Copyright rules and responsible sample culture matter. AI being involved does not absolve me of how source material is used. I'd rather use ethically developed models where practical — while accepting access to those is limited, and without turning that into a faux legal standard or a definitive “ethical AI” definition.

7. Individual capability as freedom

Individual capability is a form of freedom. Collaboration still matters, but the ability for one person to create, understand, repair and rebuild independently unlocks enormous potential. If technology makes that more accessible, ignoring it would be foolish.

8. Infrastructure and environment

My current use of external/free infrastructure is the beginning of a longer research process. The eventual goal is a more self-sustaining system with greater personal control over the infrastructure I depend upon.

AI isn't weightless. The compute, data centres, cooling and hardware all have a physical cost. Individual consumption choices are not enough to solve that. My more meaningful direction is greater technological agency, including eventually using local AI and infrastructure where practical — not pretending my current setup already gets me there.

9. Provisional

This manifesto is provisional. As AI technology evolves, its social effects become clearer, and my understanding evolves with it, the manifesto should evolve too. That's the honest position: a framework to test, not a set of commandments.

10. Purpose

deadmanifesto is my attempt to establish a personal set of rules and standards for how I use AI. It's a framework for thinking about the relationship between my creative work, AI-assisted technology and the future I want to build towards. It's also intended to make those decisions explicit enough that other people can challenge them.
