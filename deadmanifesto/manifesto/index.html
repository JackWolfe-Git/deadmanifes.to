Philosophy of the Deadman

DEADMANIFESTO

I don't want to watch the AI era happen. I want to take part in it.

1. The barrier fell

AI can already do a lot. We're still early, but the distance between an idea and a first attempt has shrunk for one person. I can start with an idea in the morning and sometimes have a working version by the evening. That doesn't mean it will be good. It means I'm more willing to try.

2. What I mean by build

Building means taking control of a system and making an idea real. Generating is different. The model produces the result, and I don't necessarily understand or control how it got there.

For me, building is a way of working with AI, not handing it the work. AI can save time on research and manual effort. I still set the goal, make the decisions and judge the result.

3. AI-assisted, AI-generated, non-AI

AI-assisted: I define the design, method or problem. The model helps me decide and carry out steps, while I set the goal and direct the workflow.

AI-generated: the model makes the finished thing from a prompt, especially video, audio or images, without me deciding how it is built.

Non-AI: no one used AI at any point.

4. Why building matters

Two projects made this concrete: the operating-system configuration I'd been thinking about for a long time, and this website, built from scratch. AI didn't make them possible by doing all the work. It made them possible because I could finally attempt things I'd wanted to do but couldn't manage alone.

5. Augmentation, not substitution

I don't want generated images, video or audio to replace human creative work. I don't want a synthetic version of a real person presented as that person. That is substitution. Generated media can be part of a human-led process. That is augmentation. The first replaces a person; the second leaves a person in charge.

6. Responsibility and attribution

Copyright and sample culture matter. Using AI doesn't excuse me from how source material is used. I'd rather use ethically developed models where I can. I accept that access to them is limited, and I don't want to turn that preference into a fake legal standard or a settled definition of “ethical AI”.

7. Individual capability

Individual capability is a form of freedom. Collaboration still matters. So does being able to create, understand, repair and rebuild on my own. If technology makes that easier, ignoring it would be foolish.

8. Infrastructure and environment

My use of external and free infrastructure is a starting point, not the end goal. I want a more self-sustaining setup with more control over what I depend on.

AI has a physical cost: compute, data centres, cooling and hardware. Individual choices won't solve that. The more useful direction is greater technological agency, including local AI and infrastructure where practical. I'm not there yet.

9. Provisional

This is a working position. As the technology and its effects change, I expect the manifesto to change with them. It should be tested, not treated as commandments.

10. Purpose

deadmanifesto is my attempt to set out rules and standards for how I use AI. It is a way of thinking about my creative work, AI-assisted technology and the future I want to build. Writing it down makes the decisions explicit enough for other people to challenge.
